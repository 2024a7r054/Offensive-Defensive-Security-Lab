# Experiment 10: Phishing Awareness Review

## Aim

Summarize the outcomes and safeguards for an authorized phishing-awareness exercise without publishing campaign setup details or personal data.

## Scope and safety

Awareness exercises require documented authorization, informed organizational oversight, and a defined participant-protection plan. Do not collect passwords or other sensitive submissions. The source screenshots were omitted because they exposed recipient information and campaign settings, including an enabled password-capture option.

## Observed outcome

The source report recorded 2 messages sent, 1 opened, 1 link clicked, 0 data submissions, and 0 reports at the time of capture. These aggregate counts are a single observation and should not be treated as a general measure of security or user behavior.

## Defensive follow-up

- Provide a respectful debrief and a clear way to report suspicious messages.
- Measure reporting and training outcomes without storing credentials or unnecessary personal data.
- Keep any exercise infrastructure restricted to authorized administrators and test participants.
- Delete test data according to the organization's retention policy.

## Conclusion

The lab exercise documented limited aggregate interaction metrics. Future awareness work should prioritize consent and oversight, avoid credential collection, and share only redacted, aggregate evidence.
